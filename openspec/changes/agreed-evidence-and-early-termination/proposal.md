## Why

Seller-initiated interruption ships in part: an interruptible VM listing, a storefront admin endpoint that shortens the capacity lease, and Alkahest splitter plumbing whose money side returns `splitter_declaration_pending`. Bare metal separately has a buyer teardown route. Nothing describes what happens after an agreement is committed in terms both payment mechanisms and every domain can share:

- Seller reclaim, buyer stop, funding exhaustion, and exceptional ends such as failed hardware have no shared model, so each is built as a separate feature and the hosted and Alkahest paths diverge. This change supplies the model and the hosted path for today's `once` parts; the rate-part early ends build on it in `spot-deals-through-arkhai-payments`.
- It is undefined which steps are protocol and which are dealings between the parties after commitment.
- An agreement does not state how a dispute about it is decided, and the evidence a dispute would need is not kept.

## What Changes

- **The protocol is what a party must do to continue:** discovery, negotiation, and the mechanism's commitment act. Everything after commitment is lifecycle, visible to arbitration only through evidence. Moving the settle contract out of core is its own change, `move-settle-out-of-core`.
- **The committed agreement is the mechanism's:** Alkahest's arbiter and `DemandData`, the hosted payments mandate, or a countersigned Agreement. The buyer verifies it is the derivation of the negotiated terms before committing. There is no universal agreement digest.
- **Each deal names its dispute authority,** an independent party like the oracle of a `TrustedOracleArbiter`: a client of the payment mechanism with its own interface, errors, fees and policies, never one system with the payments service. Under the payment mechanism, `adjudication.dispute` gives the authority's interface and its configuration (origin, account, record key, interface-owned params). Authorities advertise the payment mechanisms they act on. Default authorities come from the registry: its descriptor may recommend authorities, operator-authored, and a role's list is its own `[[Dispute]]` preferences followed by its registry's recommendations; the open-source code ships no operator's authority. A buyer accepts an authority only on an exact match of interface, origin, account and record key. A committed deal is serviced by the authority its Agreement names, with credentials bound by identity.
- **A deal is a set of parts, and payment events are per part** at the payment authority; payment adapters translate deal-level commands into them. **Disputes are per agreement and separate from the payment core:** a case is filed with the agreement's dispute authority, which answers in its own protocol, any party may append to it, and its ruling is a list of per-part effects, such as partial reverses, and optionally a delivery end, possibly several per transaction; a partial reverse is a discount while the term continues. Only the dispute authority applies outcomes; lifecycle events within the terms, such as an entitled stop, stay with the parties. Events follow each mechanism's joints: the hosted ledger reverses held payment partially, so a bond is only the seller's stake beyond it, while all-or-nothing Alkahest escrows need a separate part for any finer consequence. One dispute window in `adjudication` sets each part's earliest payout, from the part's own end, and drives evidence retention; late filings are the authority's policy. A case holds exactly the money for which the payments service accepted the dispute authority's `extend`, sent at filing and reported per part in the authority's answer; each case owns its extensions (`extend` returns a `ref`, `lift {ref}` ends one), capped by the agreed `case_hold_max_seconds` (a nonnegative integer) or unbounded (`null`), and only the dispute authority may send them. A setup `once` part ends at activation: payout is one window later, and the standard self-penalty returns all setup if undelivered and none after delivery. Each option's `params` carry `adjudication` (the dispute authority, window and case-hold cap), `early_termination` (entitlements with one notice and committed partial-period treatment, maximum term and premature_stop_penalty in asset minor units (absent/zero none); no exposure field) only where a part can be stopped, and a reserved `bond` that no hosted builder emits yet. Entitlements and the dispute authority are the stoppable parts' stop authorities. A seller's exceptional end is a dispute filing through the dispute adapter, journaled with the end, with no stop or reverse sent by the seller; Arkhai mediation's only mechanical ruling is the standard self-penalty for a payee-filed request at least as strong (stop rate parts at now returned, reverse earnings since at and the unserved share of once parts, then reverse min(premature_stop_penalty, earned amounts still held) to payer across parts in mandate order; bond award reserved for hosted bonds), and every other case, including buyer_end with acknowledgement, enters human triage. Seller stop entries exist only for entitlements. The case carries the exact Agreement verified against mandate deal hash, not a required payment attachment. The maximum term is an absolute instant shared by billing and the lease. These are common query fields filtered per option; the listing-wide interruptible flag becomes per-option Alkahest release models.
- **One lifecycle interface on each side of the mechanism.** Each party action has one counterpart, the storefront, the payment authority or the dispute authority, whose answer is the outcome. Per-part payment events and per-agreement dispute events are kept apart from buyer-to-seller peer messages (`commit`, `hint`, `end_delivery`); payment adapters resolve per mechanism through the role table and dispute adapters per dispute interface through a dispute adapter table; a dispute kit names no payment mechanism. Commands have defined outcomes and stable operation keys, and filing and `append` are idempotent; `watch` replays from a durable cursor per record; the seller can `list` its deals. Seller-authenticated and buyer-authenticated lifecycle routes expose them. The seller's refund becomes `reverse`; reconciliation stays as a backstop. This change ships the hosted payment adapter for `once` parts and the `arkhai.mediation.v1` dispute adapter; the Alkahest adapter waits for a specialized arbiter. API-credit options carry the terms and roles check them against their dispute entries, but servicing API-credit disputes is out of scope.
- **The authority's record decides.** Parties act at the authority with their own credentials; only the payee can `start` a rate part, after provisioning; hints only bring a read forward.
- **End service at T** takes final requests from authority stops, dispute outcomes that end delivery, site-signed `lease_ended` reports, and local delivery decisions (`end_delivery` in either domain, terminal settlement), earliest *T* winning and governing delivery admission before a lease exists; funding is a renewable timer, not an end (owned by `converge-ongoing-compute-leases`); a maximum-term lease is the backstop. The foundation owns revision-checked transactional `admit` and atomic `record_end(final_request, filing_intent? | pending_classification?)` and later same-journal `resolve_correction`; seller servicing budgets are recommendations, not a second notice.
- **Buyer delivery ends share one canonical-client path.** The buyer half submits the rate stop at the payment authority and sends a hint, keeping delivery until its effective time (`spot-deals-through-arkhai-payments` owns the re-drive); end_delivery has no at and records/signs storefront receipt. It ends service now but the storefront cannot stop billing for the buyer: when the authority is unreachable the buyer half journals its stop and retries under the same key, and billing runs until it lands. The buyer may additionally file buyer_end(at) with kept evidence; human triage preserves payer notice/treatment and nets recorded stops; unavailable filings are journaled and retried. VM gains an end verb and bare metal's teardown verb moves to the lifecycle client. Ending a reserved or current Alkahest deal's delivery does not invent a payment stop or refund. Ends are recorded with actor, cause and source, including before fulfillment begins, and progress never calls queued teardown released.
- **The site signs the end it intends,** atomically with the lease transition, before teardown, and delivers the immutable `lease_ended` report from an event-keyed outbox. Both storefronts receive it idempotently and return its signed bytes verbatim to the buyer through the authenticated lifecycle read; the lease read recovers a lost push. Proven capacity release remains a separate callback. This site-report boundary is independently verifiable without the storefront.
- **Each party keeps its evidence,** including the counterparty's signed message the agreement rests on, on every acceptance path, keyed by negotiation ID, in a buyer-side and a storefront-side store, until the Agreement's dispute window after the latest of the deal's delivery end, the last payout and the close of its cases. A case is one exportable payload.
- **Hosted first; a neutral trustless alternative stays possible.** Alkahest is at the user's own risk until a specialized arbiter exists.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `deal-lifecycle`: the per-part event, command and peer-message model, the lifecycle terms and their semantics, the buyer and seller halves and their routes, the evidence store, per-agreement disputes with per-deal authorities, cases and per-part outcomes, and end service.

### Modified Capabilities

- `negotiation-protocol`: the protocol ends at commitment; dispute authorities are chosen per option.
- `buyer-orchestration`: "Payment buyers approve and settle by negotiation" generalizes to verifying the mechanism's committed form; `commit` replaces `settle_agreement` where the buyer lifecycle route exists; buyer stop, dispute, and read go through the buyer half.
- `market-composition`: "Deals compose negotiate, settle, and provision stages" treats settle as the post-commitment handoff; "Marketplace roles hold no payment ledger" admits lifecycle observation cursors; "Arkhai payments authority remains external" gives cases and outcomes to the dispute authority.
- `settlement-servicing`: "The payments mandate is derived exactly from the Agreement" holds through the dispute window, an approval window apart from it, entitlement-derived stops, dispute-authority-only hold extensions, and no start or stop on `once` parts; "Only the seller reverses a held payment", retitled "Only the seller refunds a held payment", separates a full refund from a partial reverse and sends the buyer's recourse to the dispute mechanism.
- `deployment-state`: "Domain payment persistence is role-owned" gives case state to the dispute authority; "Marketplace deployment config contains consumer data only" moves dispute policy to `[[Dispute]]`.
- `test-compatibility`: "External payment qualification names its target" leaves cases and outcomes as the dispute authority's evidence.
- `settlement-configuration`: lifecycle terms are common clause fields; `[[Dispute]]` preferences and `[[DisputeCredential]]` bindings replace the payments section's dispute authority, which is rejected with a diagnostic; Alkahest release models are listed per option, and migration maps the old flag.
- `storefront-publication`: lifecycle terms, including the dispute window, are published per option; payment options bind an approval window; the admin interrupt route reads the accepted option.
- `cli-query-language`: lifecycle terms are common settlement fields.
- `registry-discovery`: a registry's descriptor may recommend dispute authorities.
- `vm-storefront-fulfillment`: "Full settlement convergence ownership" is modified, since end service writes the lease; VM gains buyer ends through the shared route, and lease ends keep second precision.
- `physical-provisioning`: site-signed `lease_ended` reports recorded at the ending transition, durable event-keyed delivery and pull recovery, distinct from proven capacity release.
- `site-capacity`: immutable intended-end records with cause and source state on every lease-ending path, without weakening capacity-release guards.

- Hosted rate parts and their early ends (`spot-deals-through-arkhai-payments`).
- Moving the settle contract out of core (`move-settle-out-of-core`).
- Lease extension (top-up).
- Delivering courtesy notices to buyer-supplied webhooks.

- Merging the VM and bare-metal domains.

## Impact

- **Wire:** lifecycle terms inside option `params` change option IDs; hosted mandates change authorities and holds, so listings re-derive them on republish; new lifecycle routes. Rollout is forward only: hosted payments has had no live use, so storefronts and buyer clients upgrade together and development deployments discard accepted hosted work.
- **Persistence:** an evidence store keyed by negotiation ID, lifecycle observation cursors, and an end-service journal per deal; site-owned immutable end reports and their event-keyed outbox. No core-owned financial end decision.
- **Packaging:** new `kit/deal-lifecycle` (foundation), `kit/deal-lifecycle-contracts`, `kit/deal-lifecycle-client`, `kit/deal-lifecycle-buyer` (buyer side), and `kit/deal-servicing` (storefront side); the hosted adapter in `kit/arkhai-payments`; the `arkhai.mediation.v1` adapter in new `kit/arkhai-mediation`, the first dispute kit; `[[Dispute]]` defaults; storefronts and buyer stages bind them through the role table and a dispute adapter table.
